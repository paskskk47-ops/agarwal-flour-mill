Hygienic Spice Grinding at Rajat Path

Turmeric is not just a coloring spice; it is an ancient Ayurvedic superfood packed with medicinal benefits, primarily due to its active compound—**Curcumin**. However, commercial packaged turmeric powder is widely compromised with toxic yellow lead-chromate dye, starch, and sawdust. Additionally, industrial milling extracts the valuable essential oils (turmeric oil) before packaging to sell separately.

At Agarawal Flour Mill, we grind dry turmeric bulbs (Sabut Haldi) using slow-grinding spice pulverizers. Our low-temperature grinding process preserves the natural oils and keeps the curcumin content fully intact, giving you healthy, aromatic, and chemical-free turmeric powder for your household.

Why Freshly Ground Haldi is Superior

The difference between commercial turmeric and our fresh ground haldi is evident immediately in color, aroma, and taste:

  • Rich Natural Curcumin: High-purity turmeric acts as a powerful anti-inflammatory and immunity-boosting agent.
  • Intense Golden Hue: Pure haldi has a deep mustard-yellow color rather than the bright chalky yellow of dyed alternatives.
  • Antiseptic & Safe: Safe to use in warm milk (Golden Milk), skincare masks, and ayurvedic remedies.
  • Natural Spice Oils: We do not extract any natural oils, ensuring the characteristic earthy and warm flavor of turmeric remains intact.

Our Spice Grinding Integrity

Dry turmeric roots are incredibly dense and tough. We use heavy-duty, water-cooled spice pulverizers to crush them effectively without generating high heat. This prevents the loss of volatile oils. Our spice grinders are cleaned and vacuum-brushed thoroughly between different spices to prevent cross-flavoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of turmeric bulbs should I bring?

You can bring standard whole dried turmeric fingers (Sabut Haldi) or premium varieties like Selam or Lakadong turmeric. Make sure they are thoroughly dry so they grind into a fine, flowable powder.

Is spice grinding done in the same machine as wheat?

No, never. We have completely separate machinery for spices and grains. Grains are ground in stone chakkis, while spices are processed in specialized stainless-steel pulverizers to keep flavors pure and prevent cross-contamination.

How much time does it take to grind turmeric?

Since dry turmeric bulbs are extremely hard, the process is slightly slower than grain grinding. Grinding 5kg of turmeric takes roughly 15-20 minutes.
